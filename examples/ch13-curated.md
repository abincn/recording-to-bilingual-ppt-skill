## Core rules (one slide)

- Start from net income.
- Add back non-cash items: depreciation, loss on disposal, bond discount amortisation.
- Current asset increase = subtract, decrease = add.
- Current liability increase = add, decrease = subtract.
- Gain on disposal is deducted in Operating; Investing records only actual cash received/paid.

## Exercise 13-3 (one slide, table)

Net income 75,800. Accounts receivable 12,500 → 17,500 (+5,000, subtract).
Inventory 44,200 → 51,650 (+7,450, subtract). Accounts payable 5,100 → 8,480
(+3,380, add). Total = 66,730.
Note: dividends payable is financing, not operating.

## Exercise 13-4, Staley Inc. (one slide, table)

Net income 400,200. Add depreciation 81,750. Add loss on disposal 22,300.
Less increase in accounts receivable 6,000. Add increase in accounts payable 4,710.
Total = 502,960.
Derivation note: depreciation 81,750 is spoken in a clearer transcription;
accounts receivable increase 6,000 is back-derived from the total
(400,200 + 81,750 + 22,300 + 4,710 − 502,960 = 6,000) and must be verified
against the textbook. An earlier draft left both rows as "—" with a footnote.

## Exercise 13-5 (one slide, table)

Net income 106,800. Add depreciation 41,700. Less increase in accounts
receivable 11,000. Add decrease in inventory 4,800. Add decrease in prepaid

## Land, investing (one slide)

Purchase cash paid 142,400 (outflow). Sale cash received 102,700. Book cost
credited 89,400. Gain 13,300 (deducted in Operating, not recorded in
Investing). Net of the two investing items = −39,700.

## Bonds, financing, exercise 13-14 (one slide)

Issued face 450,000, discount 30,000 → cash received 420,000. Redeemed face
150,000, related discount 12,000 → cash paid 138,000. Net = +282,000.
Discount amortisation is a non-cash add-back in Operating.
